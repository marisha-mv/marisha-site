Webinar funnel: the full set-up, step by step.

A webinar funnel has seven parts. Each one hands people to the next. Here is what each part has to do, and the number that tells you if it is working.

By Marisha Lakhiani·November 22, 2026·3 min read

Webinar funnels are what I audit most. When one isn't making money, the founder nearly always blames the ads. The ads are rarely the problem. The problem is one of the six steps after the click.

01The ad

Its job: get the right person to the sign-up page at a fair price.

Watch: cost per lead. In the funnels I audit, around $15 is typical for a live webinar and $20 for a recorded one. See my cost per lead guide.

02The sign-up page

Its job: turn the click into a sign-up. It needs a clear promise, who is teaching, the date and time, and a short form.

Watch: the share of visitors who sign up. In one audit, rewriting this page took cost per lead from $84 to $47.

03The confirmation page

Its job: make them want to show up. Most businesses waste this page on “you're in”. Add a short video from the host and a button to add the event to their calendar. A 90-second video here once took show-up from 26% to 47%.

04The reminders

Their job: get people in the room. Send five to seven reminders, by email and by text or WhatsApp.

Watch: show-up rate. Aim for 40% or more on a live webinar. More in why sign-ups don't show up.

05The webinar and the offer

Its job: teach something real, then make an offer people understand. Show what they get and what it is worth before the price.

Watch: how many people are still there when you make the offer, and how many buy. I look for 12–15% of attendees buying.

A 90-second video here once took show-up from 26% to 47%.

06The checkout or sales call

Its job: make paying easy. For higher prices this is a call. See checkout fixes and how to raise your close rate.

07The follow-up

Its job: bring back everyone who watched and didn't buy. This is where most funnels stop, and where the cheapest sales are. Use the 7-day follow-up sequence with a 48-hour replay.

Real example

A coaching business running weekly webinars had healthy early numbers: leads at about $15, and about a third of sign-ups showing up. It lost its money at steps 5 to 7. The webinar ended without a clear next step, so the sales calls had to do all the selling, and about 60% of the calls it booked were with people who weren't a fit. After the webinar, the follow-up was three general emails.

The fix was in those last three steps, not in the ads.

Fix them in order

Each step feeds the next. There is no point polishing the pitch if only 15% of sign-ups hear it. Find the step with the biggest gap between your number and a typical one, and start there.

Questions people ask

What is a webinar funnel?

A set of steps that takes someone from an ad to a sign-up page, to a live or recorded class, to an offer, and then through follow-up emails.

What are the stages of a webinar funnel?

The ad, the sign-up page, the confirmation page, the reminders, the webinar and offer, the checkout or sales call, and the follow-up.

How many emails should I send before a webinar?

Five to seven reminders across email and text or WhatsApp, including the day before, an hour before and when you go live.
