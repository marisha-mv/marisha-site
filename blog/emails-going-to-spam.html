Why your emails go to spam, and how to fix it.

If your reminders and offers land in spam, you paid for leads who never see them. It is the first thing I check in every audit, and one of the cheapest to fix.

By Marisha Lakhiani·November 22, 2026·3 min read

You can write the best email of your life and lose the sale because it landed in spam. Most founders I work with have no idea it is happening. Their email tool says “delivered”, and delivered only means it reached the mail server.

In one audit, fixing this alone brought back hundreds of webinar attendees per event. Nothing else changed.

How to check in ten minutes

  • Send your next campaign to your own test addresses on Gmail, Outlook and Yahoo. Look where it lands.
  • Look at open rates by provider in your email tool. If Gmail is far below the others, Gmail is filtering you.
  • Set up Google's free Postmaster Tools. It shows how Gmail rates you as a sender.

Cause 1: your sender settings were never set up

Mail providers want proof that an email really comes from you. That proof is three settings on your website address, called SPF, DKIM and DMARC. Your email tool gives you the values. You paste them in where your website address is managed.

It takes about half an hour, and it is the fix that matters most.

Cause 2: you email people who never open

If thousands of people ignore you, providers decide your emails are unwanted and filter them for everyone. Stop sending to people who haven't opened in 90 days. Send them one last email asking if they want to stay, then remove the rest.

Cause 3: you go quiet, then send a lot

Sending nothing for a month and then five emails in launch week looks like a spammer. Send something useful every week so your volume is steady. More on that in how to make money from your email list.

Cause 4: the email looks like spam

One big image with no text, link shorteners, lots of capitals and too many links all raise flags. Write emails that look like a person wrote them: mostly text, one clear link.

Cause 5: you share a sender with bad neighbours

On a shared sending address, other companies' habits affect you. Once you send to a large list, ask your email tool about a dedicated sending address, and build its volume up slowly.

What to do this week

  1. Run the ten-minute check.
  2. Add the three sender settings.
  3. Remove people who haven't opened in 90 days.
  4. Check again after two weeks.

Questions people ask

Why are my emails going to spam?

The most common reasons are missing sender settings, a list full of people who never open, sudden jumps in how much you send, and links or words that look like spam.

What are SPF, DKIM and DMARC?

Three settings on your website address that prove an email really came from you. Your email tool gives you the values and your website host is where you add them.

How do I check if my emails land in spam?

Send a campaign to test addresses on Gmail, Outlook and Yahoo, and use Google's free Postmaster Tools to watch your sender reputation.
