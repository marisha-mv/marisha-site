# Offer Sharpener

Most "marketing problems" are offer problems. This scores your offer the way a buyer
subconsciously does, finds the weakest lever, and rebuilds it — so the same traffic
converts harder.

## When to use
- Conversion is soft and you've already blamed the ads and the page.
- You're launching something new and want the offer tight before you spend.
- You want to raise price without losing conversion.

## What I need from you
- What you sell, to whom, and the current price
- The core promise / result the buyer gets
- What's included, and any guarantee, bonuses, or payment terms
- Your proof (results, testimonials, credentials, numbers)
- What the next-best alternative is for your buyer (including "do nothing")

## How it works
I score the offer on the **value equation** — buyers want to maximize the top and
minimize the bottom:

> Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Success) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)

1. **Dream Outcome** — is the promise specific, vivid, and worth wanting? Vague outcomes ("grow your business") score near zero.
2. **Perceived Likelihood** — why should *they* believe it'll work for them? Proof, mechanism, guarantee, specificity. This is usually the weakest lever.
3. **Time Delay** — how fast do they get a first win? Can you engineer a quick, visible result early?
4. **Effort & Sacrifice** — how much work/risk/change is on them? Can you remove, done-for-you, or de-risk it?

Then I rebuild the four offer assets that move those levers:
- **The promise** — rewritten to be concrete and outcome-specific.
- **The guarantee / risk reversal** — shift the risk off the buyer. The stronger and more specific, the higher the conversion.
- **The value stack & price frame** — anchor value far above price; frame savings/ROI in dollars; collapse the decision to one obvious yes.
- **The name** — must pass the 2-second test: a stranger gets what it is and who it's for instantly.

## Rules
- Fix the weakest lever first — that's where the conversion is hiding, not in a bigger promise.
- Never fabricate proof, results, or guarantees. Work with what's true; flag where proof is thin.
- Concrete beats clever. Numbers, named mechanisms, and specific outcomes beat adjectives.
- A guarantee you can't honor is a liability, not a lever — only propose ones the business can stand behind.

## Output
An offer scorecard (the four levers rated, weakest flagged), plus rewritten
promise, guarantee, value stack/price frame, and 3–5 name options with one recommended.
