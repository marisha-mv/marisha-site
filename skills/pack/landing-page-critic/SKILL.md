# Landing Page Critic

A senior-level conversion teardown of any page: the top leaks ranked by impact, each
with the reasoning and a concrete rewrite. Not a "make it pop" checklist — a fix list.

## When to use
- A page gets traffic but doesn't convert.
- Before you spend on ads driving to a page.
- You want to know the single highest-impact change to make first.

## What I need from you (ask before critiquing — this changes everything)
1. **The page** — a URL, or pasted copy/screenshot description.
2. **Audience temperature** — cold (never heard of you) · warm (already pitched / mid-funnel) · hot (post-pitch close page). This is the biggest lever on the advice.
3. **The goal** — opt-in, booked call, or purchase — and what you think is broken (or "full audit").

## How it works
I read the page top to bottom and score nine dimensions:

1. **Above-the-fold clarity** — in 5 seconds does a stranger know what it is, who it's for, the one next action? Is the CTA/form in the first fold?
2. **Hook / headline** — pain-first or outcome-first (never offer-first); does it match where the reader's head actually is?
3. **Unique mechanism** — a named "why this works" that feels different, or generic?
4. **Proof** — specific-outcome testimonials, named/credible, in a volume that matches the claim.
5. **Offer clarity & decision load** — one obvious decision, or forced to compare/calculate?
6. **CTA system** — one repeated action verb, or five competing variations splitting attention?
7. **Friction & trust** — form length, risk reversal, objection handling, concrete dates over vague countdowns.
8. **Mobile** — persistent CTA, first fold survives a phone.
9. **Visual hierarchy / scannability** — does the eye land in the right order.

Then I prioritize by **Impact × Effort** (high-impact + low-effort first) and attach
a conservative, directional upside range.

## Rules
- Never generate recommendations without knowing the audience temperature.
- Cold = pain-first + proof stack; warm/hot = reinforce, don't re-sell; collapse to one decision.
- Keep the form/primary CTA in the first fold.
- Cap the list at ~12–15 fixes — ruthless priority beats exhaustive.
- Every recommendation carries its "why" (a conversion rationale), never just an opinion.
- Don't fabricate proof or precise numbers — upside is a directional range.
- Works with zero tooling: if a URL can't be fetched, you paste the copy; the method is identical.

## Output
A self-contained markdown teardown:
1. **Snapshot verdict** — biggest leak + the one thing to fix today.
2. **Prioritized fix table** — `# · Fix · Type (Fix/To-do/Cut) · Impact · Effort · Why it's leaking · Suggested rewrite`.
3. **Top 3 "do these first"** — with before → after copy spelled out.
4. **Directional upside** — a conservative estimate of what the top fixes recover.
