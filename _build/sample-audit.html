03 · Root causes

Why buyers stopped deciding.

The pitch and the offer

The same discount had run for 18 months. The audience had seen it.
The sales page ran a countdown timer around the clock, even when no webinar was on. Buyers learned the deadline wasn't real.
The price reveal was improvised and stumbled, at the single most important moment of the webinar.
The offer was listed as features with prices, not as outcomes. People saw costs, not what they were buying.
No single belief the pitch was built to land. Every teaching moment pulled in a different direction.
The close ended by giving people permission to leave without buying.

After the webinar

Over 43,000 sign-ups and more than 10,000 attendees in one quarter didn't buy, with no escalating follow-up.
No replay. Anyone who couldn't decide live had nothing to come back to.
The code “expired tonight”, then reappeared on the sales page the next day. Follow-up emails couldn't enforce it.
Dozens of buyers asked for a longer or multi-student option in the chat. The follow-up treated it as an objection, not a sale.

Already working. Don't touch.

  • Lead cost at the low end of the benchmark
  • Average order value up 15% in a year
  • Refund rate down from about 13% to about 10%
  • A guarantee strong enough to lead the whole offer

05 · One fix in detail

Fake urgency out. Real reasons in.

Every fix in the report comes with the exact change to make. Here is the biggest one, the offer, before and after.

Before
After
“Today only” discount that comes back at every webinar.
Each webinar opens a named group with a real start date and a fixed number of places.
A countdown timer on the sales page, running all day, every day.
The deadline is the group's live kickoff call. Miss it and you wait for the next group.
One discount, one day, then silence.
A bigger discount live, a smaller one for 7 days, then full price. Each with a reason buyers can believe.
Price revealed, then features remembered afterwards.
The guarantee first, the offer as outcomes, then the price, so the number lands small.
“Whether you join or not, best of luck.”
A clear final ask, and a way to say yes in the chat.

“Same content. Same offer. Same price. The change is how the decision is framed.”

Expected effect in the original report: 1.5 to 2 times the conversion on the offer, with a 7-day window that catches the buyers who need to talk it over first.
