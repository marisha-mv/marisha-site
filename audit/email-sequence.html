Email 01Send Day 1The Reframe
Subject

The leaks your checklist couldn't show you

Alt: What the checklist couldn't tell you

A little while ago you ran your funnel through my audit checklist.

Maybe you found a weak headline. A CTA working harder than it should. A step in your opt-in quietly costing you. You fixed a couple of things, and if you're like most people who use it, your numbers nudged up.

Good. That's exactly what it's for.

But I want to be honest about what that checklist can and can't do — because the gap between the two is where most of your money is.

A checklist finds the leaks you can see. Headlines, buttons, page structure, obvious friction. Real money, but the kind you can spot yourself once someone points at it.

The leaks that cost the most don't live on a page. They live in the gaps between your steps. And here's why they're nearly impossible to catch on your own:

You measure each step in isolation. Your opt-in rate looks fine. Your show-up rate looks fine. Your close rate looks fine. Every individual number looks acceptable — so nothing waves a red flag. But a funnel doesn't add. It multiplies. And multiplication is brutal.

Watch. Say you've got a five-step funnel and each step holds onto 80% of the people. 80% sounds healthy — you'd sign off on every one of those numbers without blinking. But 80% × 80% × 80% × 80% × 80% = 33%. Two out of every three people you paid for are gone by the end, and not one step ever looked broken. The leak isn't in any single step. It's in the compounding — which is the one thing no dashboard puts in front of you.

That's what I mean by an invisible leak. It doesn't show up as a problem. It shows up as a final number that's quietly lower than it should be, and you've made peace with it, because you've never seen it any other way.

So here's something more useful than a checklist. Map it:

Write down every step of your funnel, in order. Next to each one, write the conversion rate from the step before it. Now look at the whole column at once — not each number on its own, but the slope. The single biggest drop between two steps is your first real leak, and I'd put money on it being bigger than anything the checklist surfaced. Most founders have never once looked at their funnel as a single descending column. The first time they do, they go quiet.

Go map yours. Ten minutes. It'll tell you more than a year of swapping headlines.

I can say that with some confidence, by the way, because I've built nearly every kind of funnel there is — tripwires, webinars, challenges, live summits, you name it — and tested them at scale (my best did $9M in two weeks). The three-leak pattern I'm about to walk you through didn't come out of a book. It came from watching it show up in my own funnels first.

Tomorrow: the three invisible leaks I find in almost every funnel — top, middle, and bottom — with the exact test for each, so you can pinpoint yours without guessing.

Marisha

Email 02Send Day 2The Three Leaks
Subject

Three leaks. Top, middle, bottom.

Alt: Where your money is actually leaking

Yesterday I had you map your drop-offs into one descending column. Today, the three leaks that map almost always exposes.

And I don't say “almost always” loosely. Yes, I've audited hundreds of funnels — but the part that matters more is that I've built nearly every kind there is myself. Tripwires, webinars, challenges, live summits, you name it. My best one did $9M in two weeks. Here's the humbling bit: when I first started mapping my own funnels this way, the same three leaks were sitting right there in mine. Top, middle, bottom. That's how I know they're not a you-problem. They're a funnel-problem — they show up in everyone's, including the people who teach this for a living.

Here they are, with a test for each so you can find your own numbers today.

Leak #1 — the top. You're overpaying for leads.

Most funnels I see pay around $25 to put someone at the top, when the benchmark for their type is closer to $10. That's 2.5x too much, and on a $10K/month ad budget it's $150K a year leaving quietly. Usually it's targeting that's too broad, or a creative-to-page promise that doesn't match.

Test: Last 90 days of ad spend ÷ total leads. Compare to what your funnel type should pay. More than 50% over benchmark = you have this leak.

Leak #2 — the middle. People vanish before your offer.

This is the one from yesterday, and it's the biggest. Across the funnels I audit, around 72% of registrants never reach the moment you actually pitch. They opted in. They got the confirmation. Some even showed up. Then they left before the part you brought them there for. Their acquisition cost was identical to your buyers'. Their value: zero.

Test: Offer-watchers ÷ registrants. Under 30% = leak.

Leak #3 — the bottom. Your list is doing nothing.

Most founders I audit sit on 20,000–80,000 subscribers — all paid for — and pull 0–5% of revenue from email. A healthy paid funnel does 25–40%. You already paid to acquire these people. They already raised their hands. The cost of email revenue is basically zero, yet most businesses leave 30% of annual revenue on the table because nobody owns the sequences.

Test: Last quarter's revenue vs. what's attributed to email. Under 15% = leak.

Three leaks. Top, middle, bottom. Most funnels I see have two. A surprising number have all three.

Now — here's the part that matters. Finding a leak is the easy half. You can do that with the tests above.

Quantifying it — turning “this looks low” into “this is costing you $217,000 a year” — and then ranking all of them so you know which one to fix first: that's a different job. That's the half that actually moves the revenue line, and it's the half a checklist can't do. (If you want the quantified version of your own numbers without guessing, I built a calculator that does exactly that — about three minutes, free, at marishalakhiani.com/calculator.)

But there's a deeper reason these leaks stay hidden for so long, and it has nothing to do with your skill. It's the thing almost no founder wants to hear about themselves. That's tomorrow.

Marisha

Email 03Send Day 4Inside the Bottle
Subject

You're the last person who can find them

Alt: It's not a skill gap. It's a position gap.

Yesterday I said there's a reason these leaks stay hidden that has nothing to do with skill.

Here it is: you can't read the label from inside the bottle.

The founder who built the funnel is almost always the last person who can see what's wrong with it. Not because they're not smart — usually the opposite. Because they're too close. Too attached to the copy they wrote at 1am. Too used to the numbers to notice the one that's quietly wrong. Too buried in the business to see what's right in front of them.

This isn't a flaw in you. It's physics. The same thing that makes you good at running the business — being deep inside it — is the exact thing that blinds you to its leaks.

Here's what the best operators I know do about it: they don't try to out-discipline the problem. They bring in outside eyes.

I run the funnel at Mindvalley. It does over $150M a year. And I still bring in outside experts for the specific things I can't see myself — regularly. Not because I can't do the work. Because I know I can't see my own blind spots, and pretending otherwise is how you leak money for years.

Most founders have this backwards. They treat getting audited as something you do when something's wrong — a sign of weakness. It's the opposite. Getting another trained set of eyes on your funnel is what people at the top do precisely because things are mostly working and they want the money hiding in the gaps.

So if part of you has been thinking “I should be able to figure this out myself” — let that go. You shouldn't. Nobody can, on their own funnel. That's not the standard. The standard is knowing when to hand it to someone whose entire job is seeing what you can't.

Which is the whole idea behind the Revenue Recovery Audit™. I sit with your funnel for 7 business days, find every leak, put a dollar figure on each one, and hand your team a prioritised plan they can run inside 90 days.

Tomorrow I'll show you exactly what that looked like for one founder — a real funnel, real numbers, and what three fixes did to her revenue.
